# International Rainbow Family Gathering – 2018

## Independent Event Leadership, International Coordination & Community Operations

**Founder & Event Director:** Bharat Bhushan Bungla  
**Location:** Badabe Village, Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand, India–Nepal Border  
**Event Duration:** 26 April – 2 June 2018  
**Participants:** 900+  
**International Representation:** Approximately 70–75 countries  
**Official Registration Forms Submitted:** 857

## 1. Project Overview

The International Rainbow Family Gathering 2018 was an international community gathering organised at Badabe Village in the Pithoragarh district of Uttarakhand, India.

I independently planned, organised and led the event as Founder & Event Director, with support from Ugesh ji and community volunteers.

The gathering brought together more than 900 participants from approximately 70–75 countries.

The event required extensive coordination involving local communities, government departments, security agencies, international participants, volunteers and local service providers.

My responsibilities covered overall event planning, administration, foreign participant registration, government liaison, security coordination, accommodation, transportation, logistics, volunteer management and emergency response.

## 2. Background & Community Development Initiative

My involvement in Badabe Village started before the international gathering.

In September 2017, I initiated a homestay development project with the objective of creating local economic opportunities and encouraging tourism in a village affected by migration.

The initiative involved developing seven traditional Kumaoni mud houses at separate locations within the village.

In November 2017, the first international guest, Robert Wondarer from the United States, stayed in the village.

Following this experience, I began promoting Badabe Village internationally through Facebook, community email networks and word-of-mouth communication.

This outreach eventually developed into the International Rainbow Family Gathering 2018.

## 4. Government & Administrative Coordination

The gathering involved extensive interaction with government departments and administrative authorities because of the large number of international visitors.

I coordinated with:

- Tourism Department.
- Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM).
- District Magistrate (DM).
- Food Department.
- Local Police.
- Intelligence Department.
- Army.
- Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB).

My responsibilities included administrative communication, permissions-related coordination, foreign visitor registration procedures and security-related reporting.

### Foreign Participant Registration & C-Form Management

One of my key responsibilities was personally managing the foreign participant registration and C-Form coordination process.

A mandatory arrival and registration point named **Welcome Home** was established.

Its responsibilities included:

- Receiving incoming participants.
- Collecting participant information.
- Completing C-Form documentation.
- Maintaining participant records.
- Coordinating accommodation allocation.
- Reporting and coordination with the Intelligence Department.

I personally handled the C-Form and Intelligence coordination.

A total of 857 official registration forms were submitted to the authorities.

The Welcome Home registration centre operated with a team of two Indian members and two community members across shifts.

## 5. International Outreach & Participant Management

The gathering attracted more than 900 participants from approximately 70–75 countries.

International communication and outreach were managed through:

- Facebook.
- Community email networks.
- Word-of-mouth communication.
- International community contacts.

The operational team managed arrivals, departures, registration, accommodation allocation and participant-related queries throughout the gathering.

## 6. Volunteer Management & Operational Structure

A distributed volunteer management system was established to coordinate activities across the gathering area.

Volunteer teams were deployed at:

- Main gathering site.
- Village entry and border areas.
- Three to four operational locations around the site.

Daily evening circle meetings were conducted to review operations and plan the following day.

These meetings covered:

- Incoming and departing participants.
- Volunteer availability.
- Team assignments.
- Operational requirements.
- Accommodation issues.
- Emergency preparedness.
- Next-day activities.

Teams were formed and responsibilities allocated according to daily requirements.

## 7. Emergency Response & Issue Management

A dedicated 12-member emergency response team was established.

Three vehicles remained continuously available for emergency transportation and operational requirements, with their expenses covered by the community.

I personally handled:

- Emergency situations.
- Late participant arrivals.
- Accommodation-related problems.
- Participant complaints.
- Operational disruptions.
- Coordination between volunteers and relevant teams.

This required continuous supervision and decision-making throughout the event.

## 8. Accommodation & Hospitality Management

Accommodation was organised through multiple arrangements to accommodate international participants.

These included:

- Locally developed homestays.
- Villagers' homes.
- Temporary tents.

The Welcome Home centre managed participant information and accommodation allocation.

Local households and community members also participated in providing accommodation and related services.

The arrangements created temporary economic opportunities for local residents during the gathering.
